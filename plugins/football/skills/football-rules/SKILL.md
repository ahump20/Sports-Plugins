# Football rules: NFL and NCAA

Sources: 2026 Official Playing Rules of the NFL (cited "NFL r-s-a") and 2026 NCAA Football Rules and Interpretations, May 2026 (cited "NCAA r-s-a"). Both books change every year. Anything below marked with a season is the rule as of that season; for a play from an earlier year, apply that year's rule.

## Work every rules question in this order

1. **Level and season.** NFL or NCAA, and which year. If the user doesn't say, ask, or answer both. Never carry an NFL rule into a college game or the reverse. Kickoffs, overtime, the clock and targeting all changed between 2023 and 2026.
2. **Game situation.** Quarter and time, down and distance, ball spot, score. Before or after the two-minute warning (NFL) or Two-Minute Timeout (NCAA)? Fourth down? A try? Overtime?
3. **What happened, in order.** Who had possession; whether it was a catch, muff, fumble, backward pass or forward pass; where the ball became dead and why. For a goal-line play, decide which team provided the impetus.
4. **Fouls.** Live-ball or dead-ball; before or after a change of possession; one team or both (offsetting).
5. **Enforcement spot and distance.** Previous spot, spot of foul, or succeeding spot; then half-the-distance; then the automatic first down or loss of down.
6. **Result.** Next down, distance, spot, clock status (starts on the ready signal or on the snap), and whether the period is extended.
7. **Say how sure you are.** Cite the rule. For a play that turns on official judgment, a case-book ruling, or a rule outside this skill, say so and point to the current rulebook or case book.

## Rules that hold in both codes

- **Safety vs touchback depends on impetus**, not on who last touched the ball. If your own team's impetus puts the ball behind your goal line and it dies there or out of bounds, it's a safety. If the opponent's impetus put it there, it's a touchback (NFL 3-16, 11-5, 11-6; NCAA 8-5, 8-6, 8-7).
- **Fumble out of the opponent's end zone**, forward from the field of play, is a touchback for the defense (NFL 8-7-3 Item 4; NCAA 7-2-4-c with 8-6). A forward fumble out of bounds in the field of play comes back to the spot of the fumble (NFL 8-7-3; NCAA 7-2-4-b).
- **Muff vs fumble.** A muff is touching the ball without gaining possession. A receiver who muffs a kick that has crossed the line has not fumbled. The kicking team may recover it but may not advance it. Once a receiver gains possession and then loses it, it is a fumble and anyone may advance it.
- **Half the distance.** No distance penalty may move the ball more than half the distance to the offender's goal line (NFL 14-2-1; NCAA 10-2-6, with defensive PI as the exception).
- **Offsetting fouls** replay the down (NFL 14-5-1; NCAA 10-1-4). NFL exception: 15 yards against 5 yards (5 with no automatic first down, loss of down or runoff) enforces the 15 and ignores the 5. NCAA has no such exception.
- **Free kick after a safety** comes from the kicking team's 20 and may be a punt (NFL 6-1-1, 6-1-2; NCAA 8-5-2).
- **Fair catch kick.** After a fair catch, the receivers may try a field goal from the spot with a place kick (no tee) or drop kick, worth 3. Untimed if the catch ends the half. NFL: 10-2-4, 11-4-3. NCAA: new in 2026 (2-16-11, 6-5-1-e).

## A half or game can't end on

- **NFL:** an accepted defensive live-ball foul, plus specific offensive fouls at the defense's request, a fair catch, or a double foul. The period gets one untimed down (4-8-2).
- **NCAA:** any accepted live-ball foul by either team in the 2nd or 4th quarter (the offended team may decline the extension when the offense's foul carries loss of down), offsetting fouls, an inadvertent whistle, or a fair-catch-kick election (3-2-3).

## Stats conventions

- **Sacks:** NFL subtracts sack yards from **team passing** and leaves the quarterback's individual rushing alone. NCAA charges a sack as a **rushing** attempt and yards lost, for the player and the team. College rushing totals for quarterbacks and teams are lower because of it.
- **Kneel-downs** are scored as rushing attempts for negative yards in both. This is a stat convention, not a playing rule, and hasn't been checked against the current stat manuals.
- **NFL passer rating** (0 to 158.3): four components, each clamped to [0, 2.375]:
  `a = (Cmp/Att − 0.3) × 5`, `b = (Yds/Att − 3) × 0.25`, `c = (TD/Att) × 20`, `d = 2.375 − (Int/Att × 25)`; rating = `(a+b+c+d) / 6 × 100`.
- **NCAA passing efficiency** (uncapped): `(8.4·Yds + 330·TD + 100·Cmp − 200·Int) / Att`.
- Check numbers: 20 of 30, 250 yards, 2 TD, 1 INT gives NFL 100.7 and NCAA 152.0. The two scales aren't comparable; never convert one into the other.

## When to send the user to the book

- Questions that turn on official judgment: catch versus no catch, a football move, targeting indicators, a defenseless player. Explain the criteria; don't predict the call.
- Anything marked "unverified" in the reference files.
- Seasons before 2013. High school (NFHS), FCS-specific or Division II/III quirks. CFL, UFL and other leagues. These skills don't cover them.
- Current-season mid-year interpretations and points of emphasis. Check operations.nfl.com and ncaa.org/playing-rules.
