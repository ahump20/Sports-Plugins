# NFL vs NCAA: side by side, with change log

Editions: **2026 Official Playing Rules of the National Football League** and **2026 NCAA Football Rules and Interpretations** (May 2026). A citation like "NFL 8-5" is rule–section(–article). "Unverified" means the text couldn't be confirmed against the 2026 books. Check the current rulebook before relying on it.

## Possession, catch, down

| Topic | NFL | NCAA |
|---|---|---|
| Catch elements | (a) complete control with hands or arms; (b) **both feet** or any body part other than hands completely on the ground inbounds; then (c) clearly perform an act common to the game (extend, take an additional step, tuck and turn upfield, ward off). 3-2-7 | Firm control of a ball in flight before it touches the ground; touches the ground inbounds with **any part of the body**; keeps control long enough to perform an act common to the game. 2-4-3-a |
| Going to the ground | A player who has (a) and (b) but not (c) and loses the ball when he hits the ground has no possession if the ball touches the ground before he regains control. 3-2-7 Note 2 | A player going to the ground in the act of catching must keep complete, continuous control through contact with the ground (field or end zone, and at the sideline). 2-4-3-b |
| Ball touches ground after control | — | A catch if control is maintained; slight movement isn't loss of control. 2-4-3-c, d |
| Runner down | Contacted by an opponent, and touches the ground with any part other than hands (including wrist) or feet (including ankle). 7-2-1-a. Can also declare himself down by falling or kneeling and making no effort to advance. 7-2-1-d | Any part of the ball carrier's body other than hand or foot touches the ground. **No contact needed.** 4-1-3-b. Exception: a player simulating a kick, or holding for a place kick, stays live |
| Slide | Giving himself up (7-2-1-d) | Ball dead when a ball carrier obviously begins a feet-first slide, or fakes one, at that spot. 4-1-3-r |
| Helmet off | Ball dead when a runner's helmet comes completely off. 7-2-1 (item r in the 2013 book, per footballzebras.com; the 2026 item letter is unverified) | Ball dead when the ball carrier's helmet comes completely off. 4-1-3-q |

## Scoring and tries

| Topic | NFL | NCAA |
|---|---|---|
| Try spot | Kick snapped from the defense's 15; pass or run from the 2. 11-3-1 | Snapped from the opponent's 3 (on or between the hashes, at or behind the 3). 8-3-2-c |
| Try values | TD 2, FG 1; defense returning the ball for a score gets 2. 11-3-2-b | TD 2, FG 1, safety 1 (the "one-point safety"); defense can score 2. 8-1-1, 8-3-1 |
| Try after time expires | Not attempted after a winning TD at the end of regulation (since 2018) or a TD that ends overtime | Not attempted after a TD on the last play of the 4th quarter unless it affects the outcome; a team up by 1 or 2 may forgo it. 8-3-2-a |
| Free kick after a safety | From own 20; drop kick, place kick or punt. 6-1-1-b, 6-1-2-a | From own 20; punt, drop kick or place kick. 8-5-2 |
| Touchback spot (general) | 20 (kickoff spots below) | 20, except free kicks: 25. 8-6-2 |

## Kickoffs

### NFL dynamic kickoff (adopted 2024, made permanent 2025, adjusted 2026)

- **Alignment.** The kicker is at the kicking team's 35 (20 for a safety kick); the other 10 kickers line up on the **receiving team's 40**. 6-1-2-a, b.
- **Receiving team.** Restraining line at its own 35. Setup zone between its 35 and 30. 6-1-2-c, d. Since 2026, five players must have a front foot on the restraining line (six if more than nine are in the setup zone). Up to four may be off the line in the setup zone, no more than two in each of the three areas between the sidelines and inbounds lines; with four off the line, at least one must be between the sideline and inbounds line on each side. 6-1-3.
- **Landing zone.** From the receiving team's 20 to its goal line. 6-1-2-e. Nobody but the kicker and returners may move until the ball touches the ground or a player in the landing zone or end zone. 6-1-3.
- **Short of the landing zone, or out of bounds.** Receiving team may take the ball 25 yards from the spot of the kick (its 40 on a normal kickoff; 30 yards on a safety kick), at the out-of-bounds spot, or where the ball landed short. 6-2-4.
- **Touchbacks.** 6-1-5.
  - Ball lands in the end zone and is downed there, or goes out of bounds behind the goal line: **35** (2025+; 30 in 2024).
  - Ball touches the ground or a player in the landing zone, then goes into the end zone and is downed: **20**.
  - Kickoff from the 50 after a penalty that ends in a touchback: **20** (2026+). This removes the reason to kick out of bounds deliberately.
- **Onside kick.** Must be declared to the Referee before the play clock starts, with its own setup-zone alignment. 6-1-6.
  - **2026:** either team, any time, any score.
  - **2025:** a trailing team, any time.
  - **2024:** a trailing team in the 4th quarter only.
- **Fair catch on a kickoff.** Fair catch rules cover free kicks in general (3-9, 10-2). Whether a fair-caught kickoff gets special placement under the dynamic format is **unverified**. The pre-2024 rule putting a fair-caught kickoff at the 25 belongs to the old format; don't apply it to 2024+ games.

### NCAA free kicks

- **Kickoff.** Kicking team's restraining line at its own 35; 20 after a safety. 6-1-1.
- **Formation.** All kickers except the kicker must be within 5 yards of the restraining line (6-1-2-b). At the kick, all inbounds and behind the ball, with **at least four on each side of the kicker** (6-1-2-c).
- **Kicking team touching.** Kickers can't touch the ball until it touches a receiver, or goes 10 yards and stays beyond the receivers' restraining line, or touches something beyond it. 6-1-3.
- **Out of bounds untouched.** A foul. Receiving team's options: 5 yards from the previous spot, 5 yards from the succeeding spot, or the ball 30 yards beyond the kicking team's restraining line (its own 35 on a normal kickoff). 6-2-1.
- **Ball dead in the end zone.** An untouched kick that hits the ground in the end zone is dead; touchback at the 25. 6-1-7.
- **Fair catch inside the 25.** A fair catch of a free kick behind the receiving team's 25 is placed at the 25 (since 2018). 6-5-1-a. An invalid signal on a free kick, caught behind the 25, is also placed at the 25. 6-5-3-a Exc.
- **"T" signal.** A returner's "T" signal while the kick is in the air is treated as an invalid fair catch signal. The ball is dead when caught, and caught behind the 25 it goes to the 25 (A.R. 2-8 and 6-5-1 rulings; 2025 change).
- **Clock.** Starts when the kick is legally touched in the field of play. 3-3-2-a.

## Passing fouls

| Foul | NFL | NCAA |
|---|---|---|
| Defensive PI | Spot foul, first down. In the end zone: first down at the 1, or halfway to the goal if the previous spot was inside the 2. 8-5 | Spot foul and first down if under 15 yards beyond the previous spot; 15 yards from the previous spot and first down if 15 or more. Snap on or inside the B-17 and outside the 2 with the foul on or inside the 2: ball at the 2. 7-3-8-c. Exempt from half-distance (10-2-6) |
| Offensive PI | 10 yards from the previous spot. 8-5 | 10 yards from the previous spot (2026; 15 before). 7-3-8-b |
| Intentional grounding | Loss of down and 10 yards from the previous spot, or loss of down at the spot of the pass if that is more than 10 yards back; safety if the passer's whole body and the ball are in his end zone. 8-2-1 | Loss of down at the spot. 7-3-2-h (also -f and -g for throws to conserve time) |
| Grounding exemption | Passer outside or formerly outside the pocket area; pass lands at or beyond the line of scrimmage extended, including out of bounds over the sideline. 8-2-1 Item 1 | Passer outside or formerly outside the tackle box; pass crosses or lands beyond the neutral zone extended. Applies only to the player who controlled the snap or the resulting backward pass. 7-3-2 Exc. |
| Illegal forward pass | Thrown from beyond the line: loss of down and 5 from the spot of the pass. Second forward pass, or one thrown after returning behind the line: loss of down and 5 from the previous spot. After a change of possession: 5 from the spot. 8-1-2 | 5 yards from the spot of the foul, plus loss of down if by Team A before a change of possession. 7-3-2-a to e |

## Personal fouls and player safety

| Topic | NFL | NCAA |
|---|---|---|
| Crown or helmet contact | Lowering the head to make forcible helmet contact, or butting the head or neck area with the helmet or facemask: 15 yards. 12-2-10. Defenseless player protections: 12-2-9 | **Targeting**, crown of the helmet. 9-1-3 |
| Head or neck hit on a defenseless player | 15 yards. There is no automatic ejection like NCAA targeting: disqualification is for flagrant acts (exact 12-2-9 and 12-2-10 penalty wording unverified), and fines and suspensions come from the league afterward | **Targeting** with the helmet, forearm, hand, fist, elbow or shoulder. 9-1-4. Needs at least one indicator: launch, crouch and upward thrust, leading with the helmet or arm, lowered head. When in question, it is a foul |
| Targeting penalty | — | 15 yards, automatic first down for defensive fouls, disqualification, **must be reviewed by replay** (12-3-5). **2026 one-year trial:** 1st upheld foul in a season = rest of that game only, with no second-half carryover; 2nd = rest of the game plus the first half of the next game; 3rd or later = rest of the game plus a one-game suspension. From the 2nd foul on, the conference may ask the national coordinator for video review. **Through 2025:** a second-half targeting foul also cost the first half of the next game |
| Roughing the passer | 15 yards plus automatic first down; disqualification if flagrant. 12-2-11 | 15 yards plus automatic first down (the standard personal-foul penalty under 9-1); added to the end of the run when a completed pass's run ends beyond the neutral zone with no change of possession. 9-1-9. Includes rushing unabated and hitting a passer in passing posture at the knee or below (9-1-9-b) |
| Horse-collar | Grabbing the inside back or side collar of the pads or jersey, or the nameplate area, and pulling the runner down (a foul even if only his knees buckle); 15 yards plus automatic first down. Not a foul in the pocket area or close-line play. 12-2-16 | Same grab and abrupt pull-down; **applies inside the tackle box** (2024 change). 9-1-15 |
| Hip-drop tackle | Grabbing or wrapping the runner, then unweighting by swiveling and dropping the hips onto his legs at or below the knee: 15 yards plus automatic first down. 12-2-18 (adopted March 2024) | No equivalent rule found in the 2026 book (unverified) |
| Pushing the runner | Pulling a runner is a foul; pushing isn't listed. 12-1-4. The 2025 proposal to ban the tush push needed 24 votes and failed, and none was on the 2026 agenda | Pushing the runner or the pile is legal; pulling is a 5-yard foul. 9-3-2, A.R. 9-3-2-I |

## Fumbles and kicks

| Topic | NFL | NCAA |
|---|---|---|
| Fourth-down fumble | Defense may advance. On offense, only the fumbler may recover and advance. If a teammate recovers, the ball returns to the spot of the fumble (or the recovery spot if behind it). 8-7-5 | Same idea. A teammate other than the fumbler recovering on 4th down: ball dead, returned to the spot of the fumble (or the recovery spot if behind it). 7-2-2-a Exc. 2. Also applies to a try (8-3-2-d-5) |
| After the two-minute warning | The same restriction on **every down**, in either half. 8-7-6 | **No such rule** |
| Forward fumble out of bounds | Back to the spot of the fumble, fumbling team's ball. 8-7-3 Item 3 | Same. 7-2-4-b-1 |
| Fumble through the opponent's end zone | Touchback, defense's ball. 8-7-3 Item 4 | Touchback (impetus and responsibility). 7-2-4-c, 8-6 |
| Kicking team recovers a kick beyond the line | Ball dead at the spot of recovery; no advance. 9-3-2 | Ball dead; kicking team keeps it only if it has legal possession. 6-3-6 |
| Fair catch kick | Field goal try from the spot of a fair catch; drop kick or place kick without a tee. 3-17-4, 10-2-4, 11-4-3 | Same, **new in 2026**. Head coach elects before the ready signal, irrevocably. Defense lines up 10 yards away; free-kick formation rules; 3 points; untimed if the catch ends a half. 2-16-11, 6-5-1-e |

## Replay

| Topic | NFL | NCAA |
|---|---|---|
| Coach challenges | At least two; a third if at least one succeeds. 15-1-1. **No challenges** after the two-minute warning of each half, in overtime, on scoring plays, or on tries. Those are booth reviews | Two per game; a third if either of the first two succeeds (2026). Must have a timeout; a timeout is charged only if the ruling isn't overturned. 12-5-1-b |
| Booth or automatic reviews | Replay official only: after the two-minute warning, throughout overtime, scores, tries, interceptions, fumbles recovered by the opponent or going out through the opponent's end zone, a 4th-down spot short of the line to gain, the kicking team keeping a kick, disqualifications. 15-1-2 | Replay official reviews **every play** and may stop the game before the next snap. 12-5-1-a |
| Replay assist (NFL) | Can advise on objective, clear-and-obvious aspects of a play and on game administration. 15-9. **2025 expansion:** for fouls called on the field (roughing the passer, intentional grounding, unnecessary roughness, facemask, horse-collar, tripping, roughing or running into the kicker), assist can fix objective elements; it can't add a foul that wasn't flagged. **2026:** league personnel may consult on disqualifications for flagrant football acts and non-football acts | — |
