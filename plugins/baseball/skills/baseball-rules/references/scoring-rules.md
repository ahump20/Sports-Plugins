# Official scoring (OBR 2026, Rule 9)

Scoring follows the ruling. First settle what happened on the field (SKILL.md procedure), then credit it. Most judgment calls turn on "ordinary effort"; the benefit of the doubt goes where a rule names it (to the batter on hits and on whether a bunt was a sacrifice, to the pitcher when rebuilding an inning for earned runs). Defensive indifference and choosing the winning reliever use their own tests. State which way the rule leans.

## At-bats and OBP

- No AB (9.02(a)(1)):
  - sacrifice bunt or sacrifice fly;
  - walk or HBP;
  - award of 1B for interference, obstruction or a shift violation.
- OBP (9.21(f)) = (H + BB + HBP) / (AB + BB + HBP + SF).
  - Sacrifice bunts are not in the denominator. Sacrifice flies are, so a sac fly lowers OBP.
  - Reaching on interference, obstruction or a shift violation is left out of OBP entirely (9.21(f) Comment). It is still no AB, and it still counts as a plate appearance for batting-title qualification (9.22(a)).
- Batting-title qualification (9.22): 3.1 PA per team game in MLB (502 over 162 games); 2.7 in the minors.

## Hits vs errors

- Credit a hit (9.05(a)) when any of these is true:
  - the batter reaches safely on a fair ball that settles on the ground or clears the fence before a fielder touches it;
  - the ball is hit too hard, or too slowly, to make a play with ordinary effort;
  - the ball takes an unnatural bounce, or hits the rubber or a base before a fielder touches it, so that no fielder can handle it with ordinary effort;
  - the ball reaches the outfield untouched, unless ordinary effort would have retired the batter;
  - the fair ball touches a runner or umpire (unless the runner is out for being touched by an infield fly);
  - a fielder makes a failed play on a preceding runner when the batter would have been safe anyway.
- Benefit of the doubt goes to the batter.
- No hit (9.05(b)) when any of these is true:
  - a runner is forced out, or would have been but for an error;
  - a forced runner is out on appeal for missing the first base he was forced to;
  - the pitcher, catcher or an infielder fields the ball and puts out a preceding runner trying to advance one base or return to his base, or would have but for a fielding error (a runner thrown out trying for an extra base doesn't cost the batter a hit);
  - a fielder fails to put out a preceding runner and the batter-runner could have been put out at first (9.05(b)(4));
  - the batter is out for interference, unless he would have been safe without it.
- Hit value (9.06):
  - Missing a base on appeal credits the hit up to the last base safely reached. Missing 1B means an AB and no hit.
  - Bases awarded under 5.06(b)(4) or 6.01(h) count as a hit of that value (9.06(e)).
  - Walk-off: credit only the bases the winning runner needed, and only if the batter runs them, even on an automatic extra-base award (9.06(f)).
  - An over-the-fence walk-off HR scores everyone (9.06(g)).
- Error (9.12(a)): a misplay that prolongs an at-bat or a runner's time on base, or lets a runner advance.
  - Slow handling with no mechanical misplay is not an error. The fielder does not have to touch the ball.
  - Mental mistakes are not errors, unless they lead to a physical misplay (example: throwing the ball into the stands thinking there are 3 outs).
  - No error for a pitcher failing to cover 1B, or for throwing to the wrong base.
  - Muffing a foul fly that prolongs an AB is an error. Exception: deliberately not catching a foul with R3 and fewer than 2 outs, to stop the runner from tagging.
  - Failing to tag a base or a runner is an error.
  - A throw that hits a base or runner and lets a runner advance is an error, even if it was accurate.
  - A fielder who fails to stop an accurate throw is charged with the error.
  - Only one error per wild throw (9.12(b)).
  - Interference, obstruction or a shift violation: one error on that fielder (9.12(c)). None if obstruction did not affect the play.
- No error (9.12(d)-(f)):
  - a catcher's wild throw on a steal, unless it lets the stealing runner take an extra base or lets any other runner advance;
  - a wild throw when the runner would have been safe anyway, unless any runner advances past the base a good throw would have held him to;
  - a wild throw trying to complete a double or triple play, unless any runner advances past the base a good throw would have held him to (a dropped DP throw is an error on the receiver);
  - a fielder who fumbles but recovers in time to force a runner;
  - walks, HBPs, and advances already scored as a WP or PB.
  - On ball four or a third strike that is a WP or PB with extra advances, score both.

## RBI (9.04)

- Credited for a run that scores, unaided by an error, on a hit, HR, sacrifice bunt, sacrifice fly, infield out or fielder's choice.
- Credited on a bases-loaded walk or HBP, or an interference, obstruction or shift award.
- Credited on an error, with fewer than 2 outs, on a play where the runner from third would ordinarily have scored ((a)(3)). "Never an RBI on an error" is wrong.
- Not credited on a force or reverse-force double play, or when a muffed throw at 1B would have completed a force DP ((b)).
- A fielder who holds the ball or throws to the wrong base: the scorer judges whether the runner would have scored anyway ((c)).

## Stolen bases, caught stealing, defensive indifference (9.07)

- A runner going on the pitch who advances on a WP or PB: credit an SB, not the misplay. Exception: the misplay also lets him take an extra base, or lets another runner advance ((a)).
- Catcher's wild throw on a steal: SB, no error unless the runner takes an extra base ((b)).
- A runner who escapes a rundown and reaches the next base: SB ((c)).
- Double steal with one runner thrown out: no SB for anyone ((d)).
- Overslides and is tagged out: no SB ((e)).
- Safe only because of a muffed throw: no SB. Score a caught stealing plus an error ((f)).
- Defensive indifference ((g)): the scorer credits a fielder's choice when the defense shows no interest. Factors:
  - the inning and score;
  - whether the runner was held, pickoffs, and fielder coverage;
  - strategic motives such as a 1st-and-3rd play;
  - denying a player a record.
  - A runner from 1B advancing with R3 on base is normally scored an SB.
- Caught stealing ((h)): a runner put out, or who would have been out with errorless play, while:
  - stealing;
  - picked off and trying to advance;
  - overrunning a base while stealing.
  - No CS on a WP or PB advance, an obstruction award, or batter interference.

## WP vs PB (9.13)

- Wild pitch: the pitcher's fault. A pitch too high, wide or low for the catcher to stop with ordinary effort, or one that hits the ground before reaching him, letting a runner advance.
- Passed ball: the catcher's fault. He fails to hold a pitch he should have handled with ordinary effort.
- A third strike that gets away and lets the batter reach: strikeout plus WP or PB.
- No WP or PB if the defense records an out before any runner advances. Other advances on that play become fielder's choices.

## Sacrifices (9.08)

- Sacrifice bunt: fewer than 2 outs, a runner advances, and the batter is out or would have been but for an error.
  - Not a sacrifice if the batter was bunting for a hit. Benefit of the doubt goes to the batter.
  - A failed attempt to retire a lead runner is still a sacrifice, unless ordinary effort would not have retired the batter; then it is a hit.
  - No sacrifice if any runner is put out advancing one base, or would have been but for a fielding error (9.08(c)); charge an AB.
- Sacrifice fly: fewer than 2 outs, a fly (fair or foul) caught by an outfielder or by an infielder running in the outfield, with a run scoring after the catch.
  - Also a sac fly when the ball is dropped and the runner scores, if he could have scored after a catch.

## Earned runs (9.16)

- Method: reconstruct the inning as if there had been no errors and no passed balls. Catcher's interference is excluded from "errors" here. Give the pitcher the benefit of the doubt.
- An intentional walk counts like any other walk.
- A run is earned (a) if it scores by hits, sacrifices, steals, putouts, fielder's choices, walks, HBPs, balks or wild pitches (including a third-strike WP), before fielding chances to retire the side. A defensive interference penalty counts as a fielding chance.
- Two-out error: once the reconstructed inning would have ended (the error stopped what would have been the third out), every later run is unearned. Example: two outs, an error, then home runs: 0 earned (Comment).
- Catcher's interference: the batter who reached on CI cannot score an earned run, but the scorer may not assume he would have made an out. Rule example: two outs, CI, then a HR: one earned run (the batter's).
- Not earned:
  - the runner reached on an error, on a muffed foul fly that prolonged the AB, or on interference, obstruction or a shift violation ((b));
  - his time on base was prolonged by an error ((c));
  - his advance was aided by an error, PB, interference, obstruction or shift violation, and he would not otherwise have scored ((d)).
  - A pitcher's own fielding error counts like anyone's ((e)).
- Automatic runner in extra innings: treated as reaching on an error, but no error is charged (7.01(b)(2)(C)). He is unearned. He is charged to the pitcher in the game when the half-inning starts.
- Inherited runners (9.16(g)):
  - A reliever is not charged with runners on base when he entered.
  - He is also not charged with a runner who reaches on a fielder's choice that retires a runner left by the previous pitcher.
  - Each pitcher is charged with a number of runners, not specific runners. Runners put out without action by the batter (caught stealing, picked off, interference) reduce the earlier pitcher's count. The rule gives examples (1)-(7).
- Walk counts at a pitching change (9.16(h)):

| Count when the reliever enters | Walk charged to |
|---|---|
| 2-0, 2-1, 3-0, 3-1, 3-2 | Previous pitcher |
| 2-2, 1-2, 1-1, 1-0, 0-2, 0-1 | Reliever |
| Any result other than a walk | Reliever |

- Reliever vs team (9.16(i)): the reliever gets no credit for chances to make outs that came before he entered. A run can be earned for the reliever and unearned for the team. The rule's worked examples show runs split this way, so pitcher ER totals need not add up to team ER.
- ERA (9.21(e)) = ER × 9 / IP. Example: 3 ER in 9⅓ IP = 2.89.

## Wins, losses, saves, shutouts

- Win (9.17(a)): the pitcher of record when his team takes the lead and keeps it. A tie starts a new contest.
- Starter's win (9.17(b)): the starter needs 5 complete innings in a game of 6 or more defensive innings, or 4 in a 5-inning game.
  - If he falls short, the win goes to the only reliever, or to the reliever the scorer judges most effective.
  - Comment: that reliever should normally pitch at least an inning or get crucial outs. The first reliever is not presumed; in a tie between relievers, the earlier pitcher gets it.
- Ineffective brief reliever (9.17(c)): when a later reliever pitches effectively to help hold the lead, the brief, ineffective one doesn't get the win; the most effective later reliever does. Guideline, not a requirement: under 1 inning with 2 or more earned runs, even if those runs were charged to a previous pitcher.
- Loss (9.17(d)): the pitcher charged with the go-ahead run that was never relinquished. This can be the pitcher responsible for an automatic runner.
- All-Star Game exception (9.17(e)). The 2026 WBC also uses it.
- Save (9.19). All four conditions must hold:
  1. finishing pitcher in a win;
  2. not the winning pitcher;
  3. at least 1/3 inning pitched;
  4. one of these:
     - entered with a lead of 3 runs or fewer and pitched at least 1 inning;
     - entered with the tying run on base, at bat or on deck;
     - pitched at least 3 innings.
- Shutout (9.18): a complete game, or entering with none out in the 1st before any score, retiring the side without a run and pitching the rest of the game without allowing one.
- Quality start: at least 6 IP and 3 or fewer ER. Not an official statistic; coined by John Lowe in 1985 (MLB.com glossary).
- Holds and blown saves do not appear in the OBR text; they are data-provider statistics, and their definitions vary by provider.

## Shift violation scoring summary

No AB (9.02(a)(1)(D)). Error on the fielder (9.12(c)). Ignored in OBP (9.21(f) Comment). The run is not earned (9.16(b)(2), (d)).
