# MLB edge cases (OBR 2026)

Rule numbers are OBR 2026 unless marked as an MLB regulation. Regulations (pitch timer, ABS, position-player pitching, two-way designation) are not in the OBR text; cite the announcement.

## 2026 OBR changes to know

The 2026 summary of changes includes:

- 4.03: a player not on the lineup card.
- 5.03: penalties for leaving the base-coach box.
- 5.06(b)(1): retouch responsibility, with the new Appendix 6 diagram.
- 5.10(b): signaling a pitching change.
- 5.10(g)(1) Comment: how the three-batter minimum is met.
- 5.10(g)(2) Comment: when a pitcher counts as warming up on the field.
- 5.10(h): improper substitutions. 5.10(i) is now reserved.
- 6.01(e): spectator interference on batted, thrown or pitched balls.
- 6.01(i): catcher set-up positions without the ball.
- Clarified definitions of Catch, Fair Ball, Tag and Touch. The old 5.09(a)(1) Comment and the Catch Comment moved into Definitions.

## Infield fly (Definitions, "Infield Fly")

- Conditions: R1+R2 or bases loaded, fewer than 2 outs, a fair fly that an infielder can catch with ordinary effort. Line drives and attempted bunts are excluded.
- The pitcher, the catcher, and any outfielder stationed in the infield count as infielders.
- The umpire declares "Infield Fly", or "Infield Fly, if Fair" near a line. The ball is live, and runners may advance at their own risk. A ball that goes foul is just a foul.
- Untouched and bouncing foul before 1B/3B: foul. Untouched, landing foul and bouncing fair before 1B/3B: infield fly.
- Judgment is not bounded by the grass or the baselines. It is an infield fly even if an outfielder makes the play, when an infielder could have handled it as easily. Not an appeal play.
- Because the batter is out, the force is removed. A runner who is doubled off or tagged off base is out. A runner on base cannot be forced.
- Intentional drop: the infield fly rule takes precedence over 5.09(a)(12).
- Interference during an infield fly: the ball stays live until fair or foul is known. If fair, both the runner and the batter are out. If foul, the runner is out and the batter returns to bat.
- A runner touching his base when touched by an infield fly is not out, but the batter is (5.09(b)(7) exception). Off the base, both are out.

## Batter becomes a runner / batter out

- Dropped third strike (5.05(a)(2)): the batter may run if 1B is unoccupied, or if 1B is occupied with 2 outs.
  - Otherwise he is out (5.09(a)(3)).
  - "Legally caught" means in the glove before the ball touches the ground. A ball lodged in gear or caught off the umpire does not count (5.09(a)(2)).
  - A swing at a two-strike pitch that bounced cannot be caught (5.05(a)(3)).
  - A batter-runner who clearly hinders the catcher fielding the ball is out, and runners return (6.01(a)(1)).
- Hit by pitch (5.05(b)(2)):
  - A pitch in the strike zone is a strike, whatever the batter did.
  - Outside the zone with no attempt to avoid: a ball.
  - Approved Ruling: when the batter is not awarded 1B, the ball is still dead and no runner advances.
  - A pitch that touches only the batter's jewelry is not a hit batter.
- Catcher's interference (5.05(b)(3), 6.01(c)): see the table in SKILL.md.
  - Contact before the pitch is delivered is not CI; call time and start over.
  - Squeeze or steal of home (6.01(g)): the catcher steps on or in front of the plate without the ball, or touches the batter or bat. Ruling: balk plus 1B to the batter, ball dead, the run scores.
- Runner's lane (5.09(a)(11)): both feet must be in the lane, on its lines, or on the dirt between the foul line and the grass (lane widened 2024). A run that scored on an intervening play with fewer than 2 outs, before a lane-interference out, counts (6.01(a) Penalty).
- Other batter-outs:
  - foul bunt on strike three (5.09(a)(4));
  - fair ball touches the batter before a fielder (5.09(a)(7)); a batter in the box with no intent is a foul ball instead;
  - bat hits the ball twice in fair territory (5.09(a)(8));
  - intentionally deflecting a ball over foul territory (5.09(a)(9));
  - two outs, two strikes, a runner stealing home is hit by a pitch in the zone (5.09(a)(14)): strike three, no run. With fewer than 2 outs the run counts and the ball is dead.

## Force play, time play, run scoring

- Force play (Definitions): a runner loses the right to his base because the batter became a runner.
- The force is removed when a following runner is put out (5.09(b)(6)).
- A runner who touches the base and then overslides must be tagged. A runner who retreats toward his prior base reinstates the force.
  - Comment play: R1 overslides 2B after the batter is out at 1B. That is a tag play, so a run that crossed before the tag counts.
- 5.08(a) exception: no run scores when the third out is any of these:
  1. the batter-runner before touching 1B;
  2. a runner forced out;
  3. a preceding runner declared out for missing a base.
- A runner thrown out returning to 1B after a catch is a time play: a run that crossed earlier counts.
- 5.08(b): on a game-ending bases-loaded walk or HBP, the runner from third must touch home and the batter must touch 1B.
- 5.09(d): with fewer than 2 outs, a following runner is not affected by a preceding runner's missed base.
  - If that runner's appeal out is the third out, no following runner scores.
  - If that third out is a force, no runs score at all.
- 5.08 approved rulings:
  - Batter-runner misses 1B and the appeal is the third out: no run.
  - One out, a preceding runner misses 3B on an inside-the-park HR: the following runs count.
  - Two outs, the same play: all runs are void.
  - Two outs, bases loaded, HR, batter out on appeal for missing 1B: no runs.
  - One out, R3 tags early and scores, R2 scores on a bad throw, the appeal on R3 is the third out: no runs.
- Two-out bases-loaded walk: a forced runner tagged out past his base still lets the forced run score (5.06(b)(3)(B) Comment).

## Appeals and the fourth out (5.09(c))

- Grounds:
  1. A runner fails to retouch after a catch. A flying start from behind the base is not a legal retouch.
  2. A base missed while the ball is in play.
  3. A runner overran 1B and did not return immediately.
  4. A runner missed home, made no attempt to return, and home was tagged.
- Timing: before the next pitch, play or attempted play. At the end of an inning: before the pitcher and all infielders have left fair territory.
  - An appeal is not a "play". A balk during an appeal is.
  - Time is not out during an appeal.
  - If the defense throws the ball out of play on an appeal, it may not appeal the same runner at the same base again.
- Returning to a missed base:
  - not allowed after a following runner has scored;
  - on a dead ball, not allowed once the runner has touched the next base beyond it. Example: on a HR or ground-rule double, a batter who missed 1B can go back until he touches 2B.
- Fourth out: if the third out is made during a play and an appeal on another runner is then upheld, the appeal out takes precedence.
  - With more than one appeal, the defense picks the out that helps it.
  - Comment: two runners arrive home, the first misses the plate and is out on appeal as the third out. The second run does not count.
- Batting out of turn (6.03(b)):
  - The proper batter is out on appeal, made before the first pitch to the next batter of either team, or before any play.
  - The proper batter may replace the improper one mid-count, keeping the count.
  - On appeal, every advance or run from the improper batter's ball in play, or from his reaching 1B, is erased. Advances on SB, balk, WP or PB stand.
  - A pitch to the next batter legalizes the improper batter. The order resumes after the legalized batter, or after the batter called out.
  - A proper batter who is already on base is skipped. Umpires never alert either side.
- Abandonment (5.09(b)(2)) is not an appeal play. A runner more than 3 feet from his base path avoiding a tag is out (5.09(b)(1)).

## Interference and obstruction

| Type | What it is | Ball | Penalty | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offensive (batter/runner) | Hinders a fielder or a throw | Dead | Offender out; runners return per the rule | 6.01(a) |
| Runner vs fielder on a batted ball | Fielder has right of way | Dead | Runner out, intent irrelevant; on an occupied base only if intentional, then runner and batter out (with 2 outs, the batter) | 6.01(a) Penalty Comment |
| Teammates / non-runners | Hinder a fielder | Dead | Batted ball: batter out, runners return to time-of-pitch bases. Thrown ball: the runner being played on is out | 6.01(b) |
| Catcher (defensive) | Fielder hinders the batter | Delayed | Batter to 1B; offense may take the play | 6.01(c) |
| Spectator | Fan touches a live ball | Dead | Umpire places runners to nullify; batter out if a catch was clearly prevented; R3 may score on a fly if he would have tagged | 6.01(e) |
| Coach | Accidental touch of a thrown ball | Live | No penalty. Coach physically assists a runner or interferes with a throw: runner out | 6.01(f), 6.01(a) |
| Umpire, on a catcher's throw | Hinders the throw | Dead | Runners return; ignored if the throw still retires the runner | 5.06(c)(2) |
| Umpire, on a batted ball | Fair ball hits him before touching or passing an infielder (other than the pitcher) | Dead | Batter to 1B; runners advance only if forced | 5.06(c)(6), 5.05(b)(4) |
| Authorized person (photographer, groundskeeper) | Unintentional | Live | None | 6.01(d) |
| Authorized person | Intentional | Dead | Umpire nullifies | 6.01(d) |
| Obstruction, play on runner, or batter-runner before 1B | Fielder without the ball impedes | Dead now | At least 1 base past the last base legally touched; a throw already in flight gets the wild-throw award | 6.01(h)(1) |
| Obstruction, no play | Same | Delayed | Umpire awards bases to nullify; past the protected base is at the runner's own peril | 6.01(h)(2) |

- The catcher without the ball has no right to block the base path. A fielder is no longer "in the act of fielding" once he misses the ball.
- Home-plate collision rule: 6.01(i). Catcher set-up positions were clarified in 2026.
- Bona fide slide (6.01(j)): the runner
  1. begins the slide before the base;
  2. is able and attempts to reach the base with a hand or foot;
  3. is able and attempts to stay on the base (except at home);
  4. slides within reach of the base without changing path to make contact.
  - A roll block, a kick above the knee, or throwing an arm or the upper body is not bona fide.
  - Violation: the runner and the batter-runner are both out. If the runner was already out, the runner being played on is out.
- Spectator details:
  - Spectator interference can happen on a pitched ball.
  - A ball sitting on top of the wall is live.
  - A ball a spectator touches before it enters the stands is out of play, not spectator interference.

- Equipment awards apply only if the object actually touches the ball. No penalty when a glove is knocked off by the ball or flies off during a genuine catch attempt (5.06(b)(4)(B)-(E) Comment).
- Awarded runners must still touch every base (5.06(b)(4)(I) Comment).
- Other dead-ball rulings (5.06(c)):
  - A foul tip that hits the umpire and is caught on the rebound is dead, not a catch.
  - A third strike (not a foul tip) that hits the umpire stays live.
  - A ball hidden in a uniform to deceive: the umpire calls time and advances runners at least one base.

## Balk (6.02)

- Balks with runners on (6.02(a)), the ones models miss:
  - stopping a motion to pitch; once the free foot passes the back edge of the rubber he must pitch, except to throw to 2B;
  - feinting to 1B or 3B from the rubber;
  - not stepping directly toward a base;
  - throwing or feinting to an unoccupied base except to make a play;
  - a quick pitch;
  - pitching while not facing the batter;
  - a pitching motion while off the rubber;
  - unnecessary delay;
  - standing on or astride the rubber without the ball;
  - removing a hand from the ball after coming set;
  - the ball slipping while on the rubber;
  - the catcher out of the box on an intentional walk (also 5.02(a));
  - no complete stop in the set.
- Legal moves: a feint to 2B, and a full turn and throw to 2B with a runner on 1B.
- Penalty:
  - Ignored if the batter reaches 1B and every runner advances at least one base on the pitch.
  - On a balk plus a wild throw, runners may go past the awarded base at their own risk.
  - A runner who misses the first base he advances to counts as having advanced.
- Bases empty: an illegal pitch is a ball (6.02(b)).
- A pitcher who steps back off the rubber becomes an infielder (5.07(e)).
- Stance with runners on: a pitcher in the described stance is presumed to be in the Set. He may declare the Windup only at the start of an at-bat or after a substitution or a runner advance.
- NCAA and NFHS differences: `levels.md`.

## Pace of play and roster rules (MLB)

- Pitch timer (MLB regulation, from 2023):
  - 15 s with bases empty. With runners on, 20 s in 2023, cut to 18 s in 2024. 30 s between batters.
  - Pitcher violation: a ball. He must begin his motion before the timer expires.
  - Batter violation: a strike. He must be in the box and alert with 8 s left. One batter timeout per PA.
  - Two disengagements (pickoff attempt or step-off) per PA without penalty. The third is a balk unless it produces an out. The count resets if a runner advances.
  - Mound visits, injury timeouts and offensive timeouts are not disengagements.
  - OBR 5.07(c) still carries only the old 12-second bases-empty rule; the timer lives in the regulations.
  - Exact inning-break and pitching-change clock lengths: unverified — check current rulebook. The 2024 change resets the clock to 2:00 when a new pitcher reaches the warning track with less than 2:00 left.
- ABS Challenge System (2026 regulation, not in OBR):
  - Each team gets 2 challenges and keeps one that succeeds. A team with none left gets one in each extra inning.
  - Only the pitcher, catcher or batter may challenge, immediately after the call.
  - Zone: the 17-inch plate width; top at 53.5% of the batter's height, bottom at 27%.
  - Used in the regular season and the postseason.
- Mound visits (5.10(m)):
  - 4 per team per 9 innings, plus 1 per extra inning, not carried over. A team with none left after the 8th gets one in the 9th.
  - History: 6 visits in 2018, 5 in 2019, 4 from 2024.
  - Not visits: talks between batters that don't need anyone to relocate; cleaning spikes; injury; after an offensive substitution; during a "Time" suspension; inning breaks; pitching changes.
  - With visits exhausted, a brief catcher visit may be allowed for a cross-up (5.10(m)(3)).
  - A manager who crosses the foul line with none left must change pitchers (5.10(m)(4)).
  - A second manager trip to the same pitcher in the same inning removes him (5.10(l)).
- Three-batter minimum (5.10(g)):
  - The pitcher faces 3 batters or ends the inning. The batter counts for the pitcher on the mound when the PA ends. An intentional walk counts. A pickoff does not count unless it ends the inning.
  - Unfinished batters carry into his next inning.
  - A pitcher moved to another position must satisfy it again if he returns to pitch.
  - A pitcher who warms up on the field at the start of an inning must face one batter (5.10(g)(2), from 2024).
- Shift (5.02(c), 2023):
  - When the pitcher begins his delivery and at release, at least 4 players besides P and C have both feet in front of the outer edge of the infield dirt; at release, at least 2 of them have both feet wholly on each side of 2B.
  - From the half-inning's first pitch, infielders may not switch sides except at a substitution. Bringing in a new pitcher does not count as one.
  - Penalty when the violating infielder is the first to field the ball: batter to 1B, runners up one base. Otherwise a ball is added.
  - Either way, the penalty is off if the batter reaches and every runner advances a base, and the offense may decline it.
  - Scoring: no AB, error on the fielder, run unearned, excluded from OBP.
- Larger bases: 18 in square (2.03, from 2023). This shortened 1B-2B and 2B-3B by 4.5 in.
- Automatic runner (7.01(b)(2)):
  - Each half-inning after the 9th starts with the player who batted just before the leadoff batter on 2B, or a substitute. If that player is the pitcher, the player before him may be used.
  - Permanent since 2023. Not used in the Wild Card Series, Division Series, LCS or World Series (7.01(g)).
  - The runner is treated as reaching on an error for earned runs, but no error is charged (7.01(b)(2)(C)). He is unearned and belongs to the pitcher in the game at the start of the half-inning, who can take the loss (derived from 9.16(g) and 9.17(d)).
- DH (5.11(a)):
  - The DH must bat at least once unless the opponent changes pitchers. He is locked into the batting order and may not pinch-run. A replaced DH cannot re-enter.
  - The DH role ends if any of these happens:
    - the DH takes the field;
    - the game pitcher moves to a defensive position;
    - a pinch hitter later pitches;
    - the pitcher bats or runs for the DH;
    - a fielder goes to the mound.
- Two-way "Ohtani rule" (5.11(b)):
  - A starting pitcher who also bats is listed as both SP and DH, treated as two players.
  - Removed as pitcher, he can stay as DH. Removed as DH, he can keep pitching.
  - The DH role is not lost by him batting or pitching, only by him playing a non-pitcher defensive position.
  - If replaced in both roles at once, another player cannot take over both roles.
- Two-way designation (MLB regulation): 20 MLB innings pitched and 20 games started as a position player or DH with 3 or more PA each, in the current or either of the previous two seasons.
  - Teams may carry 13 pitchers, 14 from Sept 1. Designated two-way players do not count toward that limit.
- Position players pitching (MLB regulation, 2023):
  - Allowed only in extra innings, when trailing by 8 or more, or when leading by 10 or more in the 9th. Two-way players are exempt.
  - The MLB.com glossary still shows an older "more than six runs" threshold; the 2023 rule and 2025 reporting use 8 and 10.
