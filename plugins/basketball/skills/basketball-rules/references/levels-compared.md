### Shot-clock resets in the NBA (R7-IV)

- **No reset:**
  - The defense last touches the ball before it goes out of bounds.
  - A technical or delay-of-game on the offense.
  - A held ball caused by the defense.
  - A try that misses the rim.
  - A jump-ball retoss or a suspension of play.
- **Full 24:**
  - A change of possession.
  - A personal foul or violation with a backcourt inbound.
  - A jump ball not caused by a defensive held ball.
  - Any flagrant or punching foul.
- **14:**
  - After a missed try hits the ring, or a missed free throw stays live, and the offense is first to possess.
  - A defensive loose-ball foul in that sequence with a frontcourt inbound.
  - The offense keeps the ball after it goes out of bounds in that sequence.
- **Greater of the time remaining or 14:**
  - A defensive personal foul with a frontcourt inbound.
  - Defensive three seconds.
  - A defensive technical or delay of game.
  - A kicked or punched ball in the frontcourt.

The WNBA uses the same 24/14 structure.

### Shot-clock resets in NCAA (M 2-11; W same structure)

- **Full 30:**
  - A change of control.
  - Control re-established in the backcourt.
  - A defensive foul in the backcourt.
- **20, or the time remaining if greater:**
  - A defensive personal or technical foul with a frontcourt inbound.
  - A defensive kick or fist.
- **20:**
  - An offensive rebound after the ball hits the ring.
  - Change of control with a frontcourt throw-in. Men: when the defense gets the ball on a violation. Women (2025-26): on a dead-ball change of control.
- **No reset:** the defense deflects the ball out of bounds.
- **10-second count:** Men reset it on most stoppages and always on a timeout by the team in control (M 9-10). Women do not reset it on that team's timeout.

## Timeouts and advancing the ball

- **NBA (R5-VI)**
  - 7 timeouts per team in regulation, at most 4 in Q4.
  - At most 2 after the later of the 3:00 mark of Q4 or the second mandatory timeout.
  - 2 per OT.
  - Mandatory timeouts at the first stoppage after 6:59 and 2:59 of each period.
  - **Advance:** after a timeout in the last 2:00 of Q4 or OT, with the ball in the backcourt, the team may inbound at the 28' frontcourt hash. A pass back to the backcourt gets a new 8 seconds. Once the ball has been inbounded, dribbled or passed, the option needs a new timeout.
- **WNBA**
  - 5 timeouts per team in regulation.
  - A "Reset" timeout in the last 2:00 of Q4 and of each OT.
  - 2 per OT.
  - **Advance:** 28' hash, last 2:00.
- **NCAA men (M 5-14)**
  - Media games: three 30-second and one 60-second timeout. Non-media games: four 75-second and two 30-second.
  - One extra 75-second timeout per OT.
  - A coach may request a live-ball timeout only on a throw-in, or in the last 2:00 of the 2nd half or OT.
  - **Advance:** none.
- **NCAA women (W 5-14)**
  - Media games: three 30-second and one 60-second timeout. Non-media games: three 30-second and two 60-second.
  - **Advance:** after a timeout in the last 59.9 s of Q4 or OT, the ball may be inbounded at the 28-ft line.
- **FIBA (Art. 18.2.5)**
  - 2 timeouts in the 1st half.
  - 3 in the 2nd half, at most 2 when the clock shows 2:00 or less in Q4.
  - 1 per OT. Unused timeouts do not carry over.
  - Each timeout lasts 1 minute.
  - A scoring team can't get a timeout after its own basket in the last 2:00 of Q4/OT unless play was stopped (Art. 18.2.8).
  - **Advance (Art. 17.2.4):** after a timeout taken with the ball in the backcourt in the last 2:00 of Q4/OT, the coach chooses a throw-in from the frontcourt throw-in line or from the backcourt.

## Coach's challenge and review

- **NBA (R14)**
  - One challenge per game; a second if the first succeeds.
  - Requires a timeout. A successful first challenge returns the timeout unless the timeout was also a mandatory.
  - **Challengeable:**
    - A called personal foul on the challenging team, including continuation.
    - A called out-of-bounds where that team lost possession; a challenge is the only route to review out-of-bounds.
    - A called goaltending or basket interference, outside the last 2:00 of Q4/OT, when only officials trigger review.
  - **Not challengeable:** technicals, flagrants, traveling, three seconds.
- **WNBA**
  - The same three challengeable events; officials alone review goaltending/basket interference in the last 2:00.
  - One challenge, plus a second if the first succeeds (2026 book).
- **NCAA men (M 11-4, new 2025-26)**
  - One challenge; a second if the first is overturned. Requires a timeout.
  - **Challengeable:**
    - Out-of-bounds at any time.
    - Goaltending/basket interference until the last 2:00 of regulation.
    - Secondary-defender restricted-area position until the last 2:00.
- **NCAA women (W 11-3.2/11-4)**
  - A list of coach's appeals rather than a fixed challenge count.
  - 2025-26 added out-of-bounds, backcourt violations, team control before a foul (free-throw eligibility), and correct player charged.
  - An unsuccessful appeal costs a timeout.
- **FIBA (Appendix F)**
  - Head Coach's Challenge. From OBR 2026 it may be requested at any time, and the replay system (IRS) can review goaltending/basket interference after a foul at any time.
  - Challenges per game: one (unverified — check current rulebook).

| 2025-26 | NBA | Replay center rules on the proximate foul in out-of-bounds challenges. Separately, a stat-only change: end-of-quarter heaves (Q1–Q3, last 3 s, 36+ ft from the backcourt) count as team attempts, not player attempts |
| 2025-26 | NCAA M | Coach's challenge; wider continuous motion; Flagrant 1 for groin contact; using the rim for an advantage is basket interference; one shot clock stays on if the other fails |
| 2025-26 | NCAA W | Appeals list expanded; 20-second reset on a dead-ball change of control with a frontcourt throw-in; jump stop with both feet at "approximately the same time" |
