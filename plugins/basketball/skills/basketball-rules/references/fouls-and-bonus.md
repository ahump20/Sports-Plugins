# Fouls, bonus and ejections

Editions: NBA 2026-27, WNBA 2026, NCAA M 2026-27, NCAA W 2025-26/2026-27, FIBA OBR 2026.

## Team-foul penalty ("the bonus")

| Level | Free fouls | Penalty | Resets | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NBA (R12B-V) | 4 per quarter; 3 per OT | From the 5th (OT: 4th), a non-shooting defensive foul gives 1 FT plus a penalty FT (2 total) | Each period | If a team hasn't reached its quota in the first 10 minutes of a period (OT: first 3 minutes), it may commit one foul in the last 2:00 without penalty. Flagrant, clear-path, take and away-from-the-play fouls carry their own penalties but count as team fouls |
| WNBA (R12B-V) | 4 per quarter; 3 per OT | Same as NBA | Each period | Same last-2:00 allowance (measured after the first 8 minutes of a quarter, or the first 3 of OT) |
| NCAA men (M 8-2) | 6 per half | 7th–9th: one-and-one (2nd shot only if the 1st is made); 10th+: 2 FTs | At halftime only; OT continues the 2nd half | Player-control and team-control fouls get no FTs |
| NCAA women (W 8-2) | 4 per quarter | From the 5th: 2 FTs; no one-and-one | Each quarter; OT continues Q4 | Offensive fouls get no FTs |
| FIBA (Art. 42) | 4 per quarter | From the 5th: 2 FTs on a player not shooting | Each quarter; every OT counts as Q4 (42.1.3) | Team fouls include personal, technical, disruptive, flagrant and disqualifying fouls (42.1.1). Fouls in an interval count toward the next period |

A shooting foul is administered the same way at every level, whatever the bonus: on a made basket, the basket counts plus 1 FT (the and-one); on a miss, 2 or 3 FTs by the type of try. In the NBA, a foul on a made 3 can produce a 4-point play (R12B-V).

## Fouling out and ejection

| Level | Disqualification | Ejection |
|---|---|---|
| NBA | 6th personal foul (R3-I) | 2 unsportsmanlike technicals (R12A-V); a Flagrant 2; or 2 Flagrant 1s (R12B-IV) |
| WNBA | 6th personal foul | Flagrant 2 and technical accumulation as in the NBA (unverified — check current rulebook) |
| NCAA men | 5 fouls: personal plus Class A technical; or 3 Flagrant 1s (M 4-12) | Flagrant 2; 2 Class A technicals (unverified — check current rulebook) |
| NCAA women | 5 fouls: personal, Flagrant 1 and all technicals | Flagrant 2; a 2nd technical; a 2nd Flagrant 1; or 1 technical plus 1 Flagrant 1 |
| FIBA 2026 | 5 fouls (Art. 41.1) | 2 Category 1 technicals, 2 flagrants, or one of each (Art. 41); disqualifying fouls. Disruptive fouls do not count toward disqualification |

## Flagrant fouls

- **NBA (R12B-IV)**
  - **F1:** unnecessary contact. 2 FTs plus possession.
  - **F2:** unnecessary and excessive contact. 2 FTs plus possession, and an ejection.
  - Can occur with the ball dead or alive (R4-IV(f)).
  - The shot clock resets to 24 on any flagrant (R7-IV).
- **NCAA men (M 4-15.2.c)**
  - **F1:** 2 FTs plus possession.
  - **F2:** 2 FTs plus possession, plus an ejection.
  - Examples: fouling a player clearly away from the ball to stop the clock is an F1. From 2025-26, groin contact can be an F1.
- **NCAA women:** same F1/F2 structure, with the ejection triggers in the table above.
- **FIBA 2026 (Art. 38):** a new flagrant-foul category. It counts toward disqualification. For the free-throw penalty, check Art. 38 (unverified — check current rulebook).

## Technical fouls

- **NBA (R12A-V)**
  - 1 FT, which must be shot by a player in the game (R12A-V(j)).
  - A double technical gets no FTs.
  - Defensive three seconds (R10-VII) and flopping (R10-XVII, in-game since 2023-24) are technicals but not unsportsmanlike, so they don't count toward ejection.
- **NCAA**
  - Class A (unsporting) technicals count toward the 5 personal-foul limit.
  - Class B (administrative): e.g. free-throw goaltending is 1 point plus a Class B technical (M 9-15).
- **FIBA 2026 (Art. 36)**
  - Category 1 technicals count toward disqualification. Category 2 don't.
  - Player technicals now count as team fouls.

## Transition-stopping fouls

**Clear path (NBA R12B-I penalty (6)).** Penalty: 2 FTs plus possession on the sideline. All three conditions must hold:
1. A personal foul on any offensive player during a transition scoring opportunity.
2. At the time of the foul:
   - the ball is ahead of the tip of the circle in the backcourt;
   - no defender is ahead of the player with the scoring opportunity;
   - that player has the ball, or a pass to them has been released.
3. The foul deprives the offense of a transition scoring opportunity.

It can't be a clear-path foul if the player was fouled in the act of shooting, or if the contact came from trying to intercept or deflect a pass.

**Transition take (NBA R4-IV(i), R12B-XI, from 2022-23; WNBA same).** Penalty: 1 FT, shot by any player on the court for the offended team, plus possession on the sideline. All of these must hold:
- A take foul: no play on the ball.
- It comes during a transition scoring opportunity, or right after a change of possession before the offense can advance.
- It does not meet clear-path criteria.
- It is not in the last 2:00 of Q4 or OT.

No transition opportunity exists if the offense doesn't push the pace, has slowed or stopped, runs a half-court set, or the player is fouled shooting.

**Away-from-the-play (NBA R4-IV(h), R12B-X).** Illegal defensive contact deliberately away from the immediate action in the last 2:00 of any period or OT. It also covers contact at any time before a throw-in is released. Penalty: 1 FT (any player) plus possession. If the foul is flagrant or a punch, those penalties apply instead.

**FIBA 2026 disruptive foul (Art. 37).** It replaces the unsportsmanlike foul for:
- stopping a transition attack;
- stopping the clock at the end of a quarter;
- contact from behind on a progressing player with no defender ahead of them.

It does not count toward disqualification, but it is a team foul.

**NCAA** has no transition-take rule. Deliberately fouling away from the ball to stop the clock is an F1 example (M 4-15).

## Hand-checking (NBA R12B-I(b))

Contact the defender starts on the player with the ball is illegal: forearm, hands or body check. Exceptions:
- A forearm on a back-to-the-basket player below the free-throw line extended, outside the Lower Defensive Box.
- A forearm and/or one hand with a bent elbow on a post-up player inside the Lower Defensive Box.

Incidental hand contact that doesn't affect the player's speed, quickness, balance or rhythm is not a foul. In NCAA, freedom of movement (M 10-1.4) is a 2026-27 point of emphasis. Men may use one forearm with equal pressure in the post (Major Rules Differences).
