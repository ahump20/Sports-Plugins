# Edge cases

Editions: NBA 2026-27, WNBA 2026, NCAA M 2026-27, NCAA W 2025-26/2026-27, FIBA OBR 2026. In NBA terms, a team's "own basket" is the one it attacks (R4-I).

## Goaltending vs basket interference

**NBA (R11-I).** One rule covers both. A player may not:
- touch the ball or ring while the ball is on the ring, or hang on the rim while the ball passes through;
- touch the ball above the ring inside the cylinder;
- touch a ball with a chance to score after it hits the backboard above ring level, whether it is going up or down;
- touch it on the way up after it hits the backboard below ring level;
- pin it against the backboard (a batted ball is not pinned);
- touch it on its downward flight with a chance to score;
- touch it with a hand through the ring;
- vibrate or bend the rim so the ball bounces unnaturally;
- touch the rim, net or ball while the ball is in the net.

Penalty:
- Defense violates: 2 or 3 points by the type of try. The opponents then inbound as after a made basket.
- Offense violates: no points, and the opponents get the ball at the free-throw line extended.
- Both teams violate: no points, jump ball at center.
- A player with a legal hand on the ball before it enters the cylinder may follow through.

**NCAA men (M 9-15).** The rule splits the two.
- Goaltending: a defensive player touches a try on its downward flight, above the level of the ring, with a chance to score, and not touching the cylinder.
  - If the ball has hit the backboard with any part of it above the rim, it counts as on its downward flight.
  - Touching a free throw outside the cylinder is goaltending at any point in its flight. Penalty: 1 point plus a Class B technical.
- Basket interference:
  - touching the ball or basket while the ball is on or in the basket;
  - touching the ball while any part of it is in the cylinder;
  - reaching up through the net;
  - vibrating the basket or backboard.
  - From 2025-26, using the rim to gain an advantage is also basket interference.
- Violations by both teams go to the alternating arrow.

**NCAA women (W 9-15.3).** Same structure, but goaltending requires the entire ball to be above the ring.

**FIBA (Art. 31).** The goaltending restrictions end once the ball "has touched the ring" or can no longer enter the basket (31.2.3). So after it hits the ring, any player may legally tap or block a ball rolling on the rim. This is the biggest difference from NBA and NCAA. Basket interference still applies (31.2.4):
- touching the basket or backboard while the ball is on the ring;
- reaching up through the basket;
- a defender touching the ball or basket while the ball is within the basket;
- vibrating or grasping the basket so the ball bounces unnaturally;
- grasping the basket and playing the ball.

The exception: once a whistle has blown during the act of shooting or with the ball in flight, or at the end-of-period horn, no one may touch the ball even after it hits the ring (31.2.5).

## Gather, steps and traveling

**NBA (R4-III, R10-XIII).** The gather is the moment a player gains enough control to hold, pass, shoot or change hands. For a dribbler, it is putting two hands on the ball, bringing a hand under it to a pause, or the same degree of control.
- After the gather, the player may take two steps to stop, pass or shoot. A player who hasn't dribbled may take one step before releasing.
- The first step is the first foot (or both feet) to touch down *after* the gather. A foot already down while gathering is not a step; that is the "zero step." The term itself is not in the rulebook.
- Landing on one foot makes that foot the pivot.
- A player who jumps off one foot on the first step may land on both feet for the second step, but may not then pivot.
- Other traveling violations:
  - a hop (the same foot twice);
  - sliding to gain an advantage after falling;
  - being first to touch one's own air-balled try or pass.
- Penalty: the opponents get the ball on the sideline, no closer to the baseline than the free-throw line extended.

**FIBA (Art. 25.2.1).** Same two-step count: the first step happens when a foot touches the floor "after gaining control of the ball."

**NCAA (M 9-5).** Traveling is defined by the pivot foot, with no gather or two-step language. Don't import NBA gather logic. The women's book clarifies that a jump stop lands on both feet at "approximately the same time" (2025-26).

## Continuation and and-ones

- **NBA:** on drives and other moving shots, the act of shooting begins at the gather (R4-XI).
  - A foul from then on is a shooting foul. A made basket counts plus 1 FT; the FT does not double up with the bonus (R12B-V(a)(6)).
  - Exception: in a take-foul situation with the clocks not expiring, the act of shooting starts only when the shoulders start upward. So a take foul during the gather is not a shooting foul.
  - A ball in flight before the horn counts.
  - Continuation can be challenged as part of a called foul (R14).
- **NCAA men (2025-26):** continuous motion was expanded. A dribbler who ends the dribble moving toward the basket and absorbs contact may still finish for an and-one.
- **FIBA (2026):** the act of shooting must start in the frontcourt unless it is the last action before the clock expires (Art. 15).

## End of period and the 0.3 rule

**NBA (Comments on the Rules, "Expiration of Time"; R5-III)**
- 0.3 s or more on the clocks is needed to catch and shoot.
- With 0.2 or 0.1 left, only a tip, or a high lob next to the rim that is tapped in, can score.
- When the ball is touched instantly out of bounds, at least 0.3 must run off.
- With 0.0 showing and the ball dead, the period is over.

**NCAA men (M 5-1.19)**
- With 0.3 or less on the game or shot clock, a player may score only by tapping a pass or a missed free throw.
- With under 1:00 left, at least 0.3 must run off on a deflection.
- NCAA women: (unverified — check current rulebook).

**FIBA (Art. 16.2.5)**
- 0.3 or more on the game or shot clock is needed to gain control on a throw-in or a rebound after the last free throw.
- With 0.2 or 0.1, only a tap or a direct dunk counts.

## Backcourt

**NBA (R4-VI, R10-IX)**
- The team in control may not be first to touch a ball it caused to go from frontcourt to backcourt. The opponents get the ball at midcourt.
- Exceptions: a jump ball, a try, and a tap out of a congested area while rebounding (no team control in those cases).
- Throw-ins (R8-III(e)):
  - A frontcourt or midcourt throw-in may not be passed into the backcourt, except in the last 2:00 of Q4/OT.
  - In the last 2:00, if a frontcourt throw-in is mishandled into the backcourt by the offense, the offense may not touch it first.
- 8-second count: a new 8 is given for a kicked or punched ball, a defensive foul or technical, a delay-of-game warning on the defense, a jump ball, or infection control (R10-VIII).

**NCAA men (M 9-12)**
- A pass or loose ball in the frontcourt that the defense deflects into the backcourt may be recovered by either team (9-12.5).
- A throw-in may go into the backcourt (9-12.6).
- There are jump-ball and airborne-player exceptions (9-12.8 to .10).

**FIBA (Art. 30):** (unverified — check current rulebook for the exception list).

## Throw-ins (inbound violations)

**NBA (R10-III)**
- The thrower-in has 5 seconds to release the ball. Violations:
  - stepping on the court before the release;
  - leaving the spot (one step either side);
  - touching the ball on the court before another player does;
  - throwing it straight into the basket;
  - throwing it out of bounds untouched;
  - handing it to a player on the court.
- Penalty: the opponents get the ball at the original spot.
- After a made basket, or at the start of Q2–Q4, the thrower may run the endline or pass to a teammate behind it (R8-III(c)).
- A defender who crosses the boundary before the release gets a delay-of-game warning, then technicals (R12A-II). In the last 2:00 of Q4/OT, it is an immediate technical.

**FIBA (Art. 17.3.3):** in the last 2:00 of Q4/OT, the referee first signals a warning. A defender who then crosses the boundary or crowds within 1 m on a tight sideline commits a violation that leads to a technical foul.

## Blocked shot out of bounds

There is no special blocked-shot rule.
- The ball goes to the opponents of the last player to touch it. A defender's block out of bounds is the offense's ball.
- NBA: the shot clock is not reset when the defense is last to touch (R7-IV). If the try had hit the ring first, the ring-touch reset rules apply instead.
- NCAA: no reset on a defensive deflection out of bounds (M 2-11).
- NBA: an out-of-bounds call can be reviewed only through a Coach's Challenge, at any point in the game, including the last 2:00 (R14-I(c) note).

## Jump ball vs alternating arrow

- **NBA:** jump balls at the nearest circle for a held ball, a ball sent out by both teams, or official doubt (R6-VI). Opening-tap possession sets who inbounds to start Q2–Q4 (R6-I). The WNBA follows the same start-of-period pattern.
- **NCAA (M/W 6-3, 6-4):** a jump ball to start the game and each overtime, and the arrow is set again from that tap. All other held balls and simultaneous out-of-bounds go to the alternating-possession arrow.
- **FIBA (Art. 12.6):** the same arrow system. The team that does not get first control after the opening jump gets the first arrow throw-in. The arrow holder starts the next quarter or OT from the centre line extended.

## Three seconds

- **Offensive** (all levels):
  - NBA R10-VI applies while the team controls the ball in the frontcourt. Penalty: loss of the ball. The count stops while a player is in continuous motion toward the basket.
  - NCAA men count a player as out with one foot out and the other in the air. Women require both feet outside the lane.
  - FIBA: Art. 26.
- **Defensive:** NBA and WNBA only (R10-VII). A defender may not stay in the 16-ft lane (extended 4 ft past the endline) more than 3 seconds unless actively guarding an opponent within arm's length. Penalty: a technical (1 FT); the shot clock stays the same or goes to 14, whichever is greater. NCAA and FIBA have no defensive three-second rule.

## Elam Ending

It is not in the NBA, WNBA, NCAA or FIBA playing rules for regular-season or playoff games. The All-Star Game has its own format, set each year. The 2026 NBA All-Star rules: three teams (two U.S. and one World) in a round robin of four 12-minute games, with the top two meeting in the final. There is no game clock in overtime; it is played to a target score of 5. For any later year, check the league's All-Star rules document before describing it.
