# Basketball rules

Rule answers depend on the level and the season. Most errors come from applying NBA rules to college or FIBA, or a rule's old form to the current season.

Editions checked on 2026-10-27:
- NBA 2026-27 Official Playing Rules
- WNBA 2026 Official Rule Book
- NCAA Men's Rules Book 2026-27
- NCAA Women's Rules Book 2025-26 and 2026-27 (updated Sept. 1, 2026)
- NCAA Major Rules Differences 2026-27
- FIBA Official Basketball Rules 2026 (in force Oct. 1, 2026)

Citations use NBA/WNBA "R7-IV" (rule–section), NCAA "M 9-15" / "W 8-2" (rule–section), and FIBA "Art. 29".

## Procedure

1. **Level and season.** Which league, which gender for NCAA, and which season? If not stated, ask, or answer for each level the question plausibly covers. A pre-2019 NCAA clip has a different 3-point line, and a pre-2022-23 NBA clip has no transition take foul.
2. **Live or dead ball?** Fix the state when the act happened: live, dead, in flight on a try, after the horn, or a throw-in not yet released. Many penalties change on this (away-from-the-play, flagrant on a dead ball, FIBA goaltending after the ring).
3. **Find the specific rule.** Name it and cite it. Check whether a level-specific exception applies; the table and the references list the common ones.
4. **Administer it.** State the penalty: free throws (and who may shoot), possession and the inbound spot, shot-clock value, and any foul counts (personal, team, flagrant, technical) and ejection.

When a fact is not in the references and you can't verify it, say "(unverified — check current rulebook)". Don't fill it in from memory.

## Traps

- **Bonus math.**
  - NBA/WNBA: 4 team fouls per quarter are "free"; the 5th gives 2 FTs.
  - NCAA men: one-and-one on the 7th team foul of the half, 2 FTs from the 10th. Fouls reset at halftime only.
  - NCAA women: 2 FTs from the 5th team foul of each quarter, with no one-and-one.
  - FIBA counts every OT as part of the 4th quarter. As of 2026, disruptive, flagrant and player technical fouls also count as team fouls; a technical charged to the head coach (bench) does not.
- **Fouling out.** NBA/WNBA 6; everywhere else 5.
  - NCAA men count personal fouls plus Class A technicals; NCAA women count personal fouls, Flagrant 1s and all technicals.
  - FIBA 2026: every foul a player commits (personal, disruptive, flagrant, technical) counts toward the 5 (Art. 41.1). Separately, two Category 1 technicals, two flagrants, or one of each disqualify for the game; disruptive fouls don't count toward that (Art. 36-38).
- **Goaltending vs basket interference.**
  - NBA: touching the ball on the rim, or reaching through the rim, is a violation.
  - FIBA: goaltending restrictions end once the ball touches the ring (Art. 31.2.3), so a player may legally tap a ball that is rolling on the rim. Basket interference rules still apply.
  - Credit 2 or 3 points by the type of try, not by where the touch happened.
- **Gather and steps.** NBA and FIBA allow two steps after the gather; the step taken while gathering doesn't count (the "zero step", NBA R4-III/R10-XIII, FIBA Art. 25.2.1). The NCAA rule is pivot-foot based with no gather language (M 9-5); don't import NBA gather logic into NCAA calls.
- **Shot clock.** The reset is 14 (NBA/WNBA/FIBA) or 20 (NCAA) only when the offense keeps or regains the ball after it hits the ring, or after certain frontcourt fouls and violations; a backcourt inbound or a change of possession gets a full reset. An air ball recovered by the offense gets no reset; the clock keeps running (NBA R7-IV, M 2-11).
- **Take fouls.**
  - NBA/WNBA: a transition take foul (2022-23 onward in the NBA) is 1 FT, which any player on the court may shoot, plus possession. It does not apply in the last 2:00 of Q4/OT, where away-from-the-play rules govern.
  - Clear path is a separate, harsher foul: 2 FTs plus the ball.
  - FIBA 2026 replaced the unsportsmanlike foul with the disruptive foul for these situations.
- **NCAA men's quarters.** Not adopted. 2026-27 is still two 20-minute halves (M 5-6). Don't say otherwise without a newer rulebook.
- **Elam Ending.** It is not in any league's playing rules for regular-season or playoff games, and the All-Star format changes every year. The 2026 NBA All-Star Game was a three-team round robin of four 12-minute games, with overtime played to a target score of 5 (first to reach it wins). Verify a later year's format before describing it.
